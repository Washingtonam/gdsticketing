# GDS Ticketing First-Cohort Execution Blueprint

## Objective
This is the operational playbook for the first cohort launch. The goal is to convert enrollments into approved learners, onboard them quickly, and maintain momentum during the first 30 days.

---

## 1. Launch model

The platform operates in two stages:
1. Student completes payment.
2. Admin approves access before the student enters the learning portal.

This is intentional and should be explained clearly to new learners so they understand why payment is not the same as full course access.

---

## 2. Owner execution sequence

### Step 1: Prepare the cohort list
- Confirm the exact students included in the first intake.
- Identify each student as payment-confirmed, payment-pending, or follow-up-needed.
- Prepare a short support list for all approved learners.

### Step 2: Review payment confirmations
- Go to the admin payment review area.
- Confirm each payment against the student list.
- Mark only valid payments for approval.

### Step 3: Approve access
- Approve each valid payment in the portal.
- Send the approval message immediately after approval.
- Make sure the student is directed to their dashboard and first lesson.

### Step 4: Onboard the learner
- Share onboarding instructions in a clear, simple format.
- Direct the learner to complete the first lesson within the first 24 hours.
- Ensure they know where to get support if they are blocked.

### Step 5: Monitor early engagement
- Track first-lesson completion.
- Follow up with learners who have not started.
- Capture repeated blockers and update support messaging as needed.

---

## 3. Daily launch rhythm

### Day 0: Launch prep
- Confirm access to admin dashboard.
- Verify support channels are active.
- Prepare welcome scripts and approval replies.

### Day 1: First approvals
- Approve all confirmed payments.
- Send welcome and access notifications.
- Check whether students can log in and open the learning page.

### Day 2: Early support
- Review first lesson completion.
- Send reminder messages to students who have not started.
- Resolve onboarding or login issues immediately.

### Day 3–7: Momentum building
- Follow up with learners who are inactive.
- Encourage them to complete the first module.
- Share quick wins and practical actions.

### Week 2–4: Retention and feedback
- Monitor patterns in student progress.
- Identify confusing learning steps or unclear instructions.
- Update onboarding copy and FAQ based on feedback.

## 6. Student support script

Use this script when a student asks, “Why can’t I access the course yet?”

> Your payment has been received and is under review. Access becomes available only after the admin approves your enrollment. Once approved, you will receive access to the dashboard and learning portal automatically.

Use this script when a student asks, “I paid, what happens next?”

> Your payment is recorded in the system. The next step is admin review and approval, which grants access to the learning portal.

Use this script when a student asks, “I cannot log in.”

> Please confirm your email and password. If the issue continues, contact support and we will help you access the platform.

---

## 7. Early warning signs

Watch for these issues during the first cohort:
- Students expecting immediate access after payment.
- Slow approval turnaround due to backlog.
- Students not starting after approval.
- Support messages being delayed.
- Repeated confusion around the learning portal navigation.

If these occur, respond quickly with updated messaging and direct follow-up.

---

## 8. Success measures

The first cohort is considered healthy if:
- most approved students complete the first lesson within 48 hours;
- student support replies are fast and clear;
- administration keeps approval backlog low;
- learners stay active during the first 10 days.

---

## 9. Recommended next action

The owner should begin by approving the first confirmed student wave and sending the welcome + approval flow immediately. This is the priority action that converts the platform from “ready” to “active.”
